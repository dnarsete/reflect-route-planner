How it works

1. Set your start and end. Type the address you leave from and the time you pull out. Check the box to loop back to the same place at the end of the day.

2. Load your stops. Upload an Excel file with a name, address and phone column. The app finds the columns automatically; if it guesses wrong you can remap them. Then it looks up each address on the map.

3. Plan. The planner tests thousands of possible orderings and keeps the one with the least windshield time, so more of the day is spent in front of accounts. Stops with a fixed appointment time stay locked to that time and everything else is arranged around them.

4. Drive it. Open the whole day in Google Maps, or navigate one stop at a time in Google Maps, Waze or Apple Maps — those apps carry the live traffic and re-routing.

Spreadsheet format

One row per stop. Column headers can be named anything close to these — Name, Address, Phone. Optional extras it will pick up: City, State, Zip, Minutes (time needed at that stop), Time (a fixed appointment), Notes.

Google Maps API key

Optional. Without one, the app uses a free open routing service for drive times and hands off to Google Maps or Waze for the actual navigation — that covers live traffic while you drive. Adding a key makes the planning itself traffic-aware: Google predicts what each leg will take at your departure time and sequences the day around it. A key requires a Google Cloud account with billing enabled.

Data

Everything stays in this browser — your stops, addresses and settings are saved locally on this device only. Addresses are sent to the mapping service to be located, and nothing else leaves the app.
